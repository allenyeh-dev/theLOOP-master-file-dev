## Purpose

To define SPC's weekly full inventory count procedure — providing an accurate picture of stock levels, identifying waste and shrinkage, and informing the ordering plan for the coming week.

---

## Frequency & Timing

A full inventory count is conducted once per week. The Head Chef sets the day and time — typically at a low-traffic time, often at the end of the last service of the week or on a scheduled break day.

---

## Who Conducts the Count

The Head Chef or Sous Chef leads the weekly count. A second staff member may assist. The count is not delegated to untrained line staff.

---

## Count Procedure

### Step 1 — Prepare the Count Sheet

The Head Chef maintains a master inventory list of all SPC ingredients and supplies. Before counting, prepare a count sheet with all items listed. The count sheet includes:

- Item name
- Unit of measure (kg, litres, pcs, boxes)
- Par level (the minimum required level)
- Last week's count (opening quantity)
- Deliveries received this week
- Count quantity (actual counted)
- Variance (counted vs. expected)

### Step 2 — Count All Storage Areas

Count items in this order:
1. Cold storage — all refrigerators
2. Freezer
3. Dry store
4. Any BOH storage shelving

Count every item — do not estimate. Physical count only.

### Step 3 — Record and Calculate

Enter the count quantities on the sheet. Calculate:
- **Usage this week** = Opening count + Deliveries – Closing count
- **Variance** = Expected usage (based on sales/orders) – Actual usage

Large variances (positive or negative) are flagged for investigation — they may indicate portion errors, waste, theft, or counting errors.

### Step 4 — Identify Order Needs

Based on the count, the Head Chef identifies:
- Items below par that need to be ordered before the next service
- Items with high usage that may need a higher par level
- Items with low usage that may have an overstocked par level

---

## Shrinkage & Waste Review

The weekly count provides the data to review:
- Which items have consistent unexplained variance (potential portion or waste issue)
- Whether expired item disposal (from the waste log) accounts for the variance
- Whether any items are consistently overstocked (excessive waste of shelf life)

The Head Chef uses this information to adjust par levels and ordering quantities.

---

## Count Sheet Retention

Completed weekly count sheets are retained for a minimum of 3 months. They are available for review by the F&B Director or management on request.
