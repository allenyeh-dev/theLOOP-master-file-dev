## Purpose

Cross-contamination occurs when harmful bacteria transfer from one food item, surface, or person to another. This SOP defines the controls in place at SPC to prevent cross-contamination during all kitchen operations.

---

## Cutting Board Colour Code

The single most important cross-contamination control at SPC is strict adherence to the cutting board colour code. The correct board is used for every task — no exceptions.

![Colour-coded cutting boards: WHITE / GREEN / BLUE / RED](../images/05-fsh/05-fsh_011.png)
*Caption: SPC's cutting board colour system. Four colours are in use — each dedicated to a specific food type. Using the wrong colour for any task is a food safety violation that must be corrected immediately.*

| Colour | Use |
|---|---|
| **RED** | Raw meat only (beef, pork, lamb) |
| **GREEN** | Fresh vegetables and fruits |
| **BLUE** | Raw seafood only |
| **WHITE** | Cooked food and ready-to-eat items |

**Rules:**
- Cutting boards are never used interchangeably — if the correct colour is not available, the staff member waits, cleans and sanitises an available board, or notifies the supervisor
- All cutting boards go through the dishwasher at the end of every shift
- Boards are stored dry — never stacked damp or wet
- If a board is deeply scored, stained, or warped, it is removed from service and replaced

---

## Separation of Raw and Cooked Items

- Raw proteins and cooked/ready-to-eat items are never stored on the same shelf or in the same container
- In the refrigerator: raw proteins are always on lower shelves, cooked items always on upper shelves (see SPC-FSH-003)
- During prep: the raw protein prep area and the cooked food prep area are physically separate workbenches

---

## Cleaning Between Tasks

Every time a staff member switches from a raw protein task to any other food task, they must:

1. Remove and dispose of gloves
2. Wash hands (SPC-FSH-001)
3. Clean and sanitise the workbench with a clean cloth and 75% alcohol solution
4. Put on new clean gloves
5. Collect the appropriate cutting board for the new task

This sequence is not skipped or shortened regardless of how busy service is.

---

## Surface Sanitation

![Workbench being sprayed with 75% alcohol and wiped with clean cloth while wearing gloves](../images/05-fsh/05-fsh_003.jpg)
*Caption: Workbench sanitation between tasks. The 75% alcohol solution is sprayed directly onto the surface, allowed to sit briefly, and wiped with a clean cloth. Gloves are worn throughout. A fresh cloth is used — not one that has already been used on raw protein surfaces.*

**Surface sanitation procedure:**
1. Remove any food debris from the surface
2. Apply 75% alcohol spray to the full workbench surface
3. Allow to sit for the designated dwell time (minimum 10 seconds)
4. Wipe down with a clean, dry cloth
5. Cloth is laundered or disposed of after use — not stored damp

---

## Sink Hygiene

Sinks are dedicated to their purpose and are not cross-used without cleaning between uses.

![Sink being cleaned with yellow gloves and dish soap](../images/05-fsh/05-fsh_002.jpg)
*Caption: The food prep sink is cleaned and rinsed before and after each use. Yellow gloves are worn during cleaning. Sinks that have been used for raw protein or vegetable washing are rinsed and wiped down before being used for another task.*

Sinks used for raw protein must be fully cleaned and rinsed before being used for produce washing.

---

## Staff Movement in the Kitchen

- Staff who have been handling raw proteins do not move to other stations or touch other food contact surfaces without first completing the task-switch handwashing and surface-sanitation sequence
- No food is placed directly on the floor at any time
- All dropped food is discarded — it is not picked up and returned to service
