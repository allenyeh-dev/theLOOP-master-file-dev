## Purpose

To define how equipment faults are identified, reported, and escalated at SPC — ensuring faults are addressed safely and without delay.

---

## What Counts as an Equipment Fault

An equipment fault is any situation where equipment:
- Does not start or respond as expected during the opening sequence
- Produces unusual sounds, smells, or behaviour during operation
- Fails, trips, or shuts off during service
- Shows visible damage (cracks, leaks, exposed wiring, physical damage)
- Displays an error code or warning indicator

When in doubt — report it.

---

## Immediate Action When a Fault is Discovered

1. **Stop using the equipment immediately.** Do not attempt to restart, repair, or work around the fault
2. **Isolate the equipment** where possible — if it is safe to do so, power off the unit and ensure it cannot be accidentally restarted by another staff member
3. **Notify the supervisor immediately** — verbally, face to face
4. **Do not attempt to repair or adjust the equipment yourself** — regardless of how simple the fix appears
5. **Flag the equipment** — place a clear marker (e.g., taped sign saying "OUT OF SERVICE — DO NOT USE") on the unit

---

## Gas-Related Faults

Any fault involving gas equipment (fryer, water heater, YAZAKI alarm) is treated as a high-priority safety incident:

1. **Stop using the equipment immediately**
2. **Do not attempt to relight or restart** a gas appliance that has shut off unexpectedly
3. **Notify the supervisor immediately** — they will assess whether the gas main should be closed
4. If there is any smell of gas: close the gas main valve, open windows and doors, evacuate the kitchen area, and contact the Head Chef and building management immediately

See **SPC-EQP-004 — YAZAKI Gas Cut-Off Alarm Procedure** for YAZAKI-specific alarm response.

---

## Escalation Path

| Fault Type | First Contact | If Unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| Minor equipment fault (e.g., dishwasher won't start, oven display error) | Supervisor | Head Chef |
| Equipment failure during service (e.g., fryer fails mid-service) | Supervisor → Head Chef | Building maintenance / certified technician |
| Gas-related fault or smell | Supervisor → Head Chef immediately | Building management + gas technician |
| Cold storage failure (fridge/freezer not holding temperature) | Supervisor → Head Chef | Maintenance technician — food safety action taken immediately |

---

## Fault Log

All equipment faults are logged by the supervisor in the Equipment Fault Log. Each entry records:
- Date and time
- Equipment affected
- Nature of the fault (what happened, what was observed)
- Action taken
- Who was notified
- Resolution date and what was done

The fault log is reviewed by the Head Chef monthly.

---

## Cold Storage Failures

If a refrigerator or freezer fails and cannot hold the required temperature (≤7°C / ≤-18°C):
1. Do not add new food to the unit
2. Assess the contents — items that have been out of temperature range for more than 2 hours follow the expired ingredient protocol (SPC-FSH-009)
3. Transfer salvageable items to a functioning cold storage unit
4. Notify the Head Chef — they will make the final call on what can be retained vs. discarded
5. Log all disposed food in the waste log with the reason
