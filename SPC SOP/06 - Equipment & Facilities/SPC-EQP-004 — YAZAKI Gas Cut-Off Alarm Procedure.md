## Purpose

To define the correct response when the YAZAKI gas cut-off alarm activates at SPC. The YAZAKI system is a gas leak detection and automatic shut-off device. When it alarms, it must be treated as a real gas safety event until confirmed otherwise.

---

## About the YAZAKI Gas Alarm

The YAZAKI gas cut-off alarm automatically detects gas concentration in the kitchen. When it detects gas above a threshold, it:
- Sounds an audible alarm
- Activates a visual indicator on the panel
- May automatically cut off the gas supply

*Caption: The YAZAKI alarm panel. In normal operation, the green indicator is lit and no alarm codes are displayed. When an alarm is active, the panel will show a different indicator and/or an error code. The error code reference table on or near the panel indicates the alarm type.*

---

## Normal Status — What to Check at Opening

At the start of every shift, the supervisor checks the YAZAKI panel as part of the equipment opening sequence (SPC-EQP-001):

- **Green indicator lit, no alarm codes = Normal.** Proceed with opening.
- **Red/yellow alarm indicator or any error code = Do not proceed.** Follow the alarm response below.

---

## Alarm Response Procedure

**If the YAZAKI alarm activates during a shift:**

### Step 1 — Clear the kitchen
Instruct all staff to stop what they are doing, move away from gas appliances, and step out of the immediate kitchen area. Do not panic — move calmly.

### Step 2 — Do not attempt to reset or silence the alarm immediately
The alarm should not be silenced until the cause is identified. Silencing it prematurely may mask a real gas leak.

### Step 3 — Identify the cause
Without approaching gas appliances closely, check for:
- Any gas appliance that was left on accidentally
- Any open gas valve that should be closed
- Any gas smell in the kitchen

### Step 4 — Close the gas main if any gas smell is present
If there is any smell of gas, close the gas main valve (perpendicular = closed) immediately. Do not attempt to close individual appliance valves first — go straight to the main.

### Step 5 — Ventilate
Open windows and doors. Confirm ventilation fans are running (or start them if they are not running). Do not use electric switches near the gas source if there is a strong gas smell.

### Step 6 — Contact the Head Chef
Notify the Head Chef immediately. Do not resume operations until the Head Chef has assessed the situation and authorised a restart.

### Step 7 — If gas smell persists or cause cannot be identified
If there is a persistent gas smell and the cause cannot be identified:
- Evacuate all staff from the kitchen entirely
- Do not re-enter until a qualified gas technician has inspected and cleared the area
- Contact the building manager and a licensed gas technician

---

## Resetting the YAZAKI Panel

The YAZAKI panel is reset **only after**:
- The cause of the alarm has been identified and rectified
- Any gas smell has completely cleared (typically after full ventilation of at least 15 minutes)
- The Head Chef has authorised the reset

The reset button location is specific to the SPC unit installation — the Head Chef or building manager confirms the reset procedure for the installed unit. Do not reset if the alarm re-triggers immediately after reset — this indicates an ongoing gas issue that requires a technician.

---

## False Alarms

Some YAZAKI activations are caused by non-gas sources (smoke, steam, cooking vapour in high concentrations near the sensor). If the Head Chef has confirmed there is no gas smell and no gas fault, the alarm may be treated as a false trigger. Even in this case:
- Document the event in the Equipment Fault Log
- Notify building maintenance — repeated false triggers indicate the sensor may require recalibration
- Do not permanently disable or cover the sensor
